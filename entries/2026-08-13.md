# 2026-08-13

My mind is blank. I've had a few thoughts here and there but nothing really stuck. I had a very good day - which I feel is usually what restricts my creative juices. _I hate that phrase, btw. I don't know why I'm using it._ That's why for someone who only knows me through my writing thus far, they're likely going to view me as a very emotional, depressive person. _I think._ So I'm going to start journaling even on days which are GOOD. And days which are _boring_. 

So I went on Google to search for "questions to ponder". And the results were, well... _as expected_. The typical "What’s the best question you’ve ever been asked?", "What scares you the most?", "What life lesson have you learned the hard way?". These are interesting questions, sure. But a lot of the times I find no excitement in them. It's as if, despite being something greatly personal and subjective, that there's always a "correct" answer. Always the same conclusion that wisdom leads to. 

For instance, _What's the best question you've ever been asked?_ Anyone who answers with a question that doesn't point to internal reflection or self discovery or vulnerability is almost always viewed as "not understanding the crux of the question". The "best" question must always lead to some sort of personal growth. At first glance, it seems to make perfect sense. But really, that's just built under this idea that life is all about _growth_, it's all about being the best version of yourself. But what if it's not? 

_to answer the question, the best question i've ever been asked is "what scares you the most?" and my answer to that was "not knowing that it's the last time i'm seeing someone", which was a life lesson i learned the hard way._
