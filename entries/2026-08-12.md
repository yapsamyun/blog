# 2026-08-12

_Words are powerful._ 

That's the thought that lingered in my head all the way from the station to my house. These three words made me lift the lid of my laptop - a simple task, yet in my mind it had been such a big hurdle for the past few weeks. The idea of giving myself just a few minutes on a random day to sit with my thoughts on the screen felt almost like a cheat code. It felt like I'd be giving myself an escape from this "grind" I've somehow set myself up for. It's the same feeling I get when eating ice-cream. For those few minutes, my mind blanks out and I stop all thoughts, focused solely on scooping the part that's about to melt. I've always thought it felt like a battlefield - when you have nothing but one goal for a fixed time period. Breaking out of that "zone" and stopping the momentum of the next scoop feels illegal. I don't know what will happen if I stop; I haven't thought about it - it feels illegal to even think about. 

Anyways, that's the same mode I'm in for the past few weeks. I've been in this "grind" where I cannot stop. Where it's illegal to context switch from doing something (externally) to just having thoughts. Internally. 

But back to those three words. _Words are powerful._ The internal mind is so difficult to grasp. Quite literally, given it's intangible. But the external world (ie. reality) is different. There's something for us to build off of. It's difficult going from zero to one. But not so difficult to go beyond. If I hadn't consumed the thoughts of another person in the form of writing, I would have never been able to escape the absence of conscious thought that has been haunting me for the past month. And if it weren't for these conscious thoughts, I would have never cared to formalize them in the form of my inner voice. And without my inner voice, there would be no words to map my thoughts to. And a series of "thoughts" would have never chained themselves together to lead to _"words are powerful"_. And the urge to put those down on a piece of paper (or in this case, a screen) would have never surfaced. And I would have never opened my application and started writing more words down. And my instincts to complete an entry of writing would have never kicked in, would have never prompted more thoughts. 

Words are so powerful, that I want to be capable of producing them. A lot of them. But what I see happening to myself in the past three years is the urge to feed my "ideas" into GenAI and wield this power for free. I have sold the novelty of thought in the name of "quality of expression". I have mistaken GenAI as a tool of expression, without seeing its true identity as a tool of convergence. Converging to the "correct" thoughts in the world, collectively speaking. 

In seeking perfection - perfection in grammar, perfection in style, in poetry, in "neatness" - I traded _real_ perfection. Because our thoughts are never "neat". They are never perfectly isolated to one particular question. Thoughts branch out. They fall off midway. They are RAW. Seeking to "perfect" them is but doing justice. 

That said, with my journal, I will present my thoughts as they are. Without any "outsourcing". Without any "edits". Just thoughts. On paper.
